# S1 — The Role of Strategy

> **Provenance key:** `[PN]` your 2026 notes · `[SD]` 2024–25 Studocu notes (paraphrased) · `[TB]` standard textbook theory (Grant & Jordan) not found in your notes · `[REC]` reconstructed analysis · ⚠ verify against slides

## 1. Learning objectives
- Define strategy and distinguish it from tactics.
- Explain the four elements of a successful strategy and the four roles strategy plays.
- Distinguish corporate strategy (*where* to compete) from business strategy (*how* to compete).
- Describe strategy as positioning vs. strategy as direction.
- Explain mission, vision, values, and the shareholder vs. stakeholder debate.

## 2. Core definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Strategy** | An overall plan for deploying resources to achieve long-term goals and establish a favourable competitive position. `[PN]` |
| **Chandler's definition** | The determination of long-run goals and objectives, together with the courses of action and allocation of resources needed to achieve them. `[SD]` |
| **Andrews' definition** | The pattern of objectives and major policies that defines what business the company is (or should be) in and what kind of company it is (or should be). `[SD]` |
| **Tactic** | A specific, short-term action that carries out part of a strategy. `[PN]` |
| **Strategic decision** | A decision that is important, commits substantial resources, and is difficult to reverse. `[PN]` |
| **Corporate strategy** | Decides *where* the firm competes: which industries and markets (the business portfolio). Linked to **industry attractiveness**. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Business strategy** | Decides *how* the firm competes within an industry: creating and sustaining **competitive advantage**. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Mission** | Why the organisation exists: what it does, for whom, and with what know-how. `[SD]` |
| **Vision (strategic intent)** | What the organisation wants to become in the future. `[SD]` |
| **Values** | How the organisation behaves. `[SD]` |

## 3. Concepts explained

### 3.1 The four elements of a successful strategy `[PN]` (Grant)
1. **Simple, consistent, long-term goals.**
2. **Profound understanding of the competitive environment.**
3. **Objective appraisal of resources.**
4. **Effective implementation.**

These are the "common threads" in successful strategies. Elements 2 and 3 anticipate the two halves of the course: external analysis (S2–S4) and internal analysis (S5).

### 3.2 Strategy as the link between firm and environment `[SD]`
Strategy connects the **firm** (goals and values, resources and capabilities, structure and systems) with its **industry environment** (competitors, customers, suppliers). A strategy is good when it fits both.

### 3.3 The four roles of strategy `[PN][SD]`
| Role | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Decision support** | Improves the quality of decisions by simplifying choices and focusing analysis. |
| **Coordinating device** | Creates consistency and unity across the organisation. |
| **Target** | Improves performance by setting ambitious aspirations. |
| **Animation / orientation** | Motivates and mobilises people. |

### 3.4 Strategy as positioning vs. strategy as direction `[SD]`
- **Positioning (competing for the present):** *Where* are we competing? *How* are we competing?
- **Direction (preparing for the future):** What do we want to become? What do we want to achieve? How will we get there?

### 3.5 Profits and purpose `[SD]`
- **Shareholder view.** Milton Friedman's 1970 *New York Times* essay argued that the social responsibility of business is to increase its profits.
- **Stakeholder view.** R. Edward Freeman (1984) argued that firms must consider everyone who affects or is affected by them.
- Profit is necessary for the firm to survive, but it is not the firm's reason for existing (the "breathing" analogy).
- Firms take on wider responsibilities for **ethical** reasons and out of **self-interest**:
  - **Sustainability** of the ecosystem the firm depends on.
  - **Reputation.**
  - **Licence to operate.**
- Benefit corporations and B Corps are a link forward to S16–S17.

### 3.6 Growth pathways: Ansoff matrix `[PN]`
|  | Existing product | New product |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing market** | Market penetration | Product development |
| **New market** | Market development | Diversification |

Risk increases as the firm moves away from existing products and markets; diversification is the riskiest path.

## 6. Comparisons
| A | B |
|---|---|
| Strategy: overall plan, long-term, hard to reverse | Tactic: specific action, short-term, adjustable |
| Corporate strategy: WHERE to compete → industry attractiveness | Business strategy: HOW to compete → competitive advantage |
| Mission: why we exist (present) | Vision: what we want to become (future) |
| Shareholder view (Friedman) | Stakeholder view (Freeman) |
| Positioning: competing for the present | Direction: preparing for the future |

## 8. Practical application: how to use S1 in a case answer
Open any case answer by naming the level of strategy involved:
- Is the question about **where** to compete (entering an industry or country)? That is corporate strategy.
- Is it about **how** to compete? That is business strategy.

Then test the firm's strategy against the **four elements**:
1. Are the goals clear?
2. Does the firm understand its environment?
3. Is its view of its resources realistic?
4. Can it implement the strategy?

## 9. Exam traps
- Tactics are not "small strategies". The difference is commitment and reversibility.
- Corporate strategy is not "the strategy of a corporation". It is about the **scope** of businesses.
- The mission describes the present purpose; the vision describes the future. They are easily swapped in MCQs.
- Stakeholder theory does not reject profit. It rejects profit as the *only* objective.
- In the Ansoff matrix, "market development" means a new market for an existing product, not a new product.

2. **"Distinguish corporate and business strategy, with examples."**
   - Corporate strategy: where to compete, the portfolio, linked to industry attractiveness.
   - Business strategy: how to compete, linked to competitive advantage.
   - Example: BEKO choosing European markets (corporate) vs. how it competes on price and quality within them (business). `[REC]`
3. **"Shareholder vs. stakeholder perspective: should firms pursue purpose beyond profit?"**
   - Present Friedman and Freeman.
   - Give the self-interest arguments: sustainability, reputation, licence to operate.
   - Bridge to B Corps.
