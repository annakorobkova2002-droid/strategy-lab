# S22 — Strategic Consulting (+ Course Recap)

> ⚠ **Status.** Neither set of notes covers this session. Everything below is **standard consulting methodology** `[TB]` and **reconstructed analysis** `[REC]`, chosen because it is the most likely content of a McKinsey guest session and because it helps you **structure exam answers**. Nothing is attributed to the speaker.

## 1. Learning objectives
- Explain what strategy consultants do and why firms hire them.
- Apply structured problem solving: SCQ, issue trees, MECE, hypothesis-driven work, 80/20.
- Communicate with the Pyramid Principle.
- Know classic consulting frameworks (McKinsey 7S, Three Horizons) and how course frameworks fit together.
- Use a consulting-style template to answer case questions in the exam.

## 2. Core definitions `[TB]`
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Strategy consulting** | Professional advice to top management on strategic decisions: growth, market entry, M&A, turnaround, operating models. |
| **SCQ** (situation–complication–question) | A way to frame a problem: the stable situation, what has changed, and the key question that follows. |
| **Issue tree** | Breaking a key question into sub-questions or drivers in a logical hierarchy. |
| **MECE** | **M**utually **E**xclusive, **C**ollectively **E**xhaustive: no overlaps, no gaps. |
| **Hypothesis-driven approach** | Starting from a provisional answer and testing it with targeted analysis, rather than "boiling the ocean". |
| **80/20 rule** (Pareto) | Focus on the roughly 20% of analysis that drives about 80% of the insight. |
| **Pyramid Principle** (Barbara Minto) | Communicate top-down: the answer first, then grouped supporting arguments, then the data. |

## 3. Concepts explained

### 3.1 Why firms hire consultants `[TB]`
- **Expertise:** industry and functional know-how, benchmarks.
- **Capacity:** extra analytical resources for a limited time.
- **Objectivity:** an outside view; it challenges internal inertia (links to S18).
- **Legitimacy:** support for difficult decisions (restructuring, M&A).
- **Typical projects:** growth strategy, market entry (S12), pricing, cost reduction (S6), turnaround (S18), digital and AI transformation, sustainability strategy (S16).

**Industry structure** `[TB]`:
- **strategy firms**, including the "MBB" (McKinsey, BCG, Bain);
- the Big Four's advisory arms;
- boutiques and specialists.

The pyramid career model runs from analyst to partner. The business model rests on human capital, reputation and client relationships (link to S5 and S14).

### 3.2 Structured problem solving `[TB]`
1. **Define the problem** (SCQ). Make it specific, measurable, actionable and time-bound. *Example: "How can company X restore profitability to 8% EBIT margin within 3 years?"*
2. **Structure it** (issue tree, MECE). *Profit = revenue − costs; revenue = volume × price; volume = market size × share…*
3. **Prioritise** (80/20): focus on the branches with the biggest impact.
4. **Form hypotheses and plan the analysis:** "Profit fell because price competition from low-cost entrants eroded share in segment Y."
5. **Analyse:** data, interviews, benchmarks; confirm or reject each hypothesis.
6. **Synthesise:** the "so what?" answer.
7. **Communicate** (Pyramid Principle) and **implement**.

**A MECE example: why is profit falling?**
- Revenue ↓
  - Volume ↓ (market ↓? share ↓?)
  - Price ↓ (mix? discounts?)
- Costs ↑
  - Fixed costs ↑
  - Variable costs ↑ (inputs? efficiency?)

### 3.3 Classic consulting frameworks `[TB]`
- **McKinsey 7S:**
  - "hard" elements: **strategy, structure, systems**;
  - "soft" elements: **shared values, style, staff, skills**.
  - All seven must be aligned for strategy to work (links to implementation in S1 and inertia in S18).
- **McKinsey Three Horizons of growth:**
  - H1: defend and extend the core;
  - H2: build emerging businesses;
  - H3: create options for the future.
  - Link to S11 (real options) and S9 (blue oceans).
- **Profit tree, market-sizing logic, BCG growth-share matrix** (useful for portfolio questions beyond the attending syllabus).

### 3.5 A consulting-style template for exam case answers `[REC]`
1. **Answer first** (Pyramid Principle): one sentence with your recommendation.
2. **Situation and problem** (SCQ): two or three lines of context.
3. **Analysis with named frameworks:** choose 2–3 relevant frameworks, apply them **to the case data**, and label your conclusions.
4. **Options:** 2–3 alternatives, judged on fit with the firm's resources, the environment and the risks.
5. **Recommendation and implementation:** actions, risks and mitigations, and KPIs.
6. **Theory vocabulary** throughout: define each concept once, then use it.

## 5. Exam traps
- **MECE:** "ME" means no overlap, "CE" means nothing missing. Both are needed.
- **Hypothesis-driven** doesn't mean biased; hypotheses are tested and can be rejected.
- **Pyramid Principle:** conclusion **first**, not last.
- **7S:** the soft "S"s (shared values, style, staff, skills) matter as much as the hard ones.

## 6. Likely open questions
1. "Describe the structured problem-solving approach used by strategy consultants."
2. "What is MECE? Build an issue tree for a falling-profit problem."
3. "Explain the McKinsey 7S model and why alignment matters for implementation."
4. "As a consultant, advise company X on [entry / turnaround / attack]," using the template in section 3.5.

## 7. Memorise this
- **SCQ → issue tree (MECE) → 80/20 → hypotheses → analysis → synthesis → pyramid.**
- **MECE = no overlaps, no gaps.**
- **Pyramid Principle = answer first** (Minto).
- **7S:** strategy, structure, systems + shared values, style, staff, skills.
- **Three Horizons:** core · emerging · options.
- **Exam answer:** answer → SCQ → 2–3 frameworks applied → options → recommendation + risks + KPIs.
