# S16 — Sustainability Strategies

## 1. Learning objectives
- Define corporate sustainability and the Triple Bottom Line.
- Explain why sustainability has become strategic (megatrends, global warming, overshoot, selective sustainability).
- Identify external and internal drivers, and distinguish moral obligation from the business case.
- Compare the four approaches to sustainability (passive → proactive), with automotive examples.
- Apply the four competitive sustainability strategies and sustainable value innovation.
- Explain the circular economy and its nine strategies.
- Distinguish B Corp certification from Società Benefit status.

## 2. Core definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Corporate sustainability** | Meeting the needs of a firm's direct and indirect stakeholders (shareholders, employees, clients, communities…) without compromising its ability to meet the needs of future stakeholders. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Triple Bottom Line** (3Ps) | Economic (profit) · environmental (planet) · social (people). `[PN]` |
| **Earth Overshoot Day** | The date when humanity has used all the resources Earth can regenerate in that year. `[PN]` |
| **Carbon tunnel vision** | Focusing only on carbon emissions while ignoring other problems (poverty, inequality, biodiversity, resource scarcity). `[PN]` |
| **Circular economy** | A systemic economic model, regenerative by design, that keeps products and materials in use and **decouples** growth from the consumption of finite resources. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Decoupling** | Growing economically without a proportional increase in resource use. `[PN]` |
| **B Corp** | A **voluntary certification** awarded by **B Lab** for verified social and environmental performance. `[PN]` |
| **Società Benefit** | An Italian **legal status** that writes common-benefit objectives into the company's corporate purpose. `[PN]` |

## 3. Concepts explained

### 3.1 Why sustainability matters `[PN][SD]`
**A global megatrend**, alongside digitalisation and AI, geopolitical conflict, protectionism, migration and population ageing.

**Three pressures:**
- **environmental:** climate change effects;
- **economic:** scarce resources become expensive;
- **social:** inequality follows.

**Global warming and the firm** `[SD]`:
- higher costs;
- supply-chain disruption;
- lost markets and assets.
- Exposed sectors: tourism, agriculture, insurance.

**Ecological debt:** an earlier Overshoot Day means more pressure on the planet. `[PN]`

**Selective sustainability:** "nothing has zero impact".
- EV batteries depend on minerals mined under harmful conditions.
- Solar farms compete with land and water needed by local people.
- Fast fashion and planned obsolescence in electronics push hidden costs onto society.

**Exam point:** a product can help in one dimension while harming another.

### 3.2 Drivers `[PN][SD]`
| External drivers | Internal drivers |
|---|---|
| Government regulation | Top-management commitment (owners with a moral drive) |
| Responsible investors (less sustainable firms carry a higher risk premium) | Employee motivation and purpose |
| Customer and citizen awareness, activists | Cost savings and efficiency |
| Stakeholder pressure (suppliers, communities) | Reputation |
| Competitors as benchmarks (Patagonia, Lush) `[SD]` | Differentiation |
| | New consumer needs and segments (for example, solid shampoo attracting sensitive customers `[SD]`) |

**Four groups push firms** `[PN]`: governments and institutions, investors, consumers, and firms themselves.

**Two motivations:**
- **Moral obligation:** doing the right thing.
- **Business case:** creating economic value through efficiency, innovation and differentiation.

They can coexist.

### 3.3 Four approaches to sustainability: the automotive example `[PN][SD]`
**Context:** EU **Euro emission standards** (Euro 3 in 2001, 4 in 2006, 5 in 2011, 6 in 2016) were announced in advance. `[SD]`

| Approach | Motivation | Goal | Example | What it did |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Passive** | Regulation | Cost minimisation | **Hummer** (spelt "Hammer" in the notes) | Left Europe for markets without such rules (Middle East, China); later failed as fuel prices rose `[SD]` |
| **Reactive** | Political pressure | Compliance | **Renault** | Adapted engines year by year to each new norm, spreading the costs `[SD]` |
| **Adaptive** | Regulation, market sensitivity, public opinion | Business optimisation | **Toyota** (Prius) | Turned regulation into an opportunity: hybrid technology, efficiency, low running costs, a head start `[SD]` |
| **Proactive** | Competitive opportunity, CSR, long-term strategy | Transforming strategy and mission | **Tesla** | Created a new zero-emission market as its core business `[SD]` |

**Key distinction:**
- **Passive and reactive:** sustainability as **compliance**.
- **Adaptive:** sustainability as **optimisation**.
- **Proactive:** sustainability as **competitive advantage**.

### 3.4 Competitive sustainability strategies `[PN][SD]`
Two dimensions: **competitive advantage** (lower cost vs. differentiation) × **competitive focus** (organisational processes vs. products and services).

| | **Organisational processes** | **Products and services** |
|---|---|---|
| **Lower costs** | **Socio-environmental efficiency:** cut energy, water and waste in operations. *Online banking: less paper, fewer branches* `[SD]` | **Sustainable cost leadership:** sustainable products at a lower cost. *Unes water bottles held by a neck ring instead of full shrink-wrap* `[SD]`; redesigned packaging |
| **Differentiation** | **Sustainable process differentiation:** customers value *how* you produce. *Certified organic farming* `[SD]`; responsible, transparent production | **Sustainable branding:** sustainability is the brand. *NaturaSì* `[SD]`; products from sustainable materials |

- **Cost strategies** raise profit through lower cost at the same price.
- **Differentiation strategies** raise profit only if the **premium exceeds the extra cost**.

**Fifth strategy: sustainable value innovation** `[PN][SD]`:
- at the same time it **cuts economic costs and environmental impact** and **raises customer value and contribution to society**;
- it is the Blue Ocean logic applied to sustainability;
- **IKEA:** flat packaging means fewer trucks and lower emissions; efficiency offsets the extra cost of sustainable wood; affordable variety.

### 3.5 Circular economy `[PN][SD]`
**Linear:** take → make → use → waste. **Circular:** keep products and materials in use (reuse, repair, recycle).

**Nine strategies in three categories** `[PN]` (degree of circularity and business-model integration falls from top to bottom):
| Category | Strategy | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Intelligent product rethinking** (highest) | Transformation | Radically redesign products, processes or the way needs are met | Replace a resource-intensive product with a different solution |
| | Sharing | Increase use by giving many users access | **Rent the Runway** (clothing rental) |
| | Reduction | Use fewer materials, more efficiently | Remove non-essential packaging |
| **B. Extension of useful life** | Reuse | Use again, for the same or a new purpose | Second-hand furniture |
| | Repair | Fix damaged products | **Patagonia Worn Wear**; "Don't Buy This Jacket" |
| | Renewal | Restore or refurbish | Refurbished electronics |
| | Rework | Rework components into renewed products | Remanufactured parts |
| **C. Useful destination of materials** (lowest) | Recycling | Turn waste into new material | **Nike** shirts from plastic bottles |
| | Recovery | Turn waste into energy | Waste-to-energy |

> ⚠ The 2024–25 notes label category C differently ("rethinking… radical business model innovation"). Your 2026 notes are internally consistent, so they are used here (audit C3).

**The circular economy is not just recycling.** Recycling and recovery are the *least* circular strategies.

**Difficulties** `[SD]`:
- recycling start-ups face uncertain input volumes, R&D costs to keep quality, and the need for a **reverse supply chain**;
- life-extension models can **cannibalise** revenue, so firms need new revenue streams.

### 3.6 B Corp vs. Società Benefit `[PN]`
| B Corp | Società Benefit |
|---|---|
| A **certification** | A **legal status** |
| Awarded by **B Lab** after an impact assessment | Recognised by **national law** (Italy) |
| Voluntary, performance-based | Common-benefit purpose written into the bylaws |

- A company can be a Società Benefit **without** B Corp certification, and vice versa. The two are related but not the same.
- **Three principles:** **purpose** (positive impact), **responsibility** (consider all stakeholders), **transparency** (make impact visible).
- **Five assessment areas:** governance, workers, community, customers, environment.

**Examples** `[PN]`:
- **WAMI** (Water With A Mission): each bottle sold funds about 100 litres of drinking water.
- **Greyston Bakery:** **open hiring**, housing, community services.
- **Ben & Jerry's:** Caring Dairy (farmer training), ICE Academy (training for marginalised people), its Foundation.

## 5. Exam traps
- **B Corp = certification; Società Benefit = legal status.** This is the classic trap.
- **Recycling ≠ circular economy.** It is the *least* circular strategy.
- **Adaptive ≠ proactive:** adaptive *optimises* the existing business; proactive *transforms* strategy and mission.
- **Socio-environmental efficiency** (processes, cost) vs. **sustainable cost leadership** (products, cost).
- Sustainable differentiation pays only if the **premium exceeds the extra cost**.

## 7. Memorise this
- **CS = current + future stakeholders. TBL = economic · environmental · social.**
- **Drivers:** external (regulation, investors, customers, stakeholders) vs. internal (management, employees, efficiency, reputation, differentiation, new needs). **Moral obligation vs. business case.**
- **Approaches:** passive (Hummer) → reactive (Renault) → adaptive (Toyota) → proactive (Tesla).
- **Matrix:** socio-environmental efficiency · sustainable cost leadership · sustainable process differentiation · sustainable branding. **+ sustainable value innovation (IKEA).**
- **Circular (9):** transformation, sharing, reduction · reuse, repair, renewal, rework · recycling, recovery.
- **B Corp (certification, B Lab) ≠ Società Benefit (legal status).** **Purpose · responsibility · transparency.**
