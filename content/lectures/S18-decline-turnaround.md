# S18 — Organizational Decline and Corporate Turnaround

## 1. Learning objectives
- Define organisational decline and identify its causes.
- Explain why organisational change is difficult: inertia and technological change.
- Describe the five steps of a turnaround.
- Distinguish portfolio, financial and organisational restructuring, and their outcomes.
- Apply the concepts to Nokia and BlackBerry.

## 2. Core definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organisational decline** | An **involuntary** process in which a firm's performance deteriorates and its resource base erodes. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Organisational inertia** | An organisation's inability or reluctance to adapt to changing conditions. `[PN]` |
| **Corporate turnaround** | The process of reversing decline and restoring competitiveness and profitability. `[PN]` |
| **Downsizing** | Reducing the number of employees, and sometimes operating units, which may or may not change the business portfolio. It is **intentional and proactive**. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Downscoping** | Divesting, spinning off or otherwise eliminating businesses unrelated to the core, so the firm **refocuses** on its core. `[PN][SD]` |
| **LBO** (leveraged buyout) | Acquisition financed mostly with **borrowed** funds. `[PN][SD]` |
| **MBO** (management buyout) | Acquisition of a business by its existing managers. `[PN]` |

## 3. Concepts explained

### 3.1 Causes of decline `[PN][SD]`
| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Poor management | Incompetence, neglect |
| Overexpansion | Empire-building CEOs, excessive growth and diversification |
| Inadequate financial controls | No clear profit responsibility |
| High costs | Low productivity, inefficiency |
| New competition | Powerful emerging rivals |
| Unforeseen demand shifts | Major market changes |
| Organisational inertia | Slow response to new competitive conditions |

### 3.2 Nokia `[PN][SD]`
- In **2006** Nokia held about **31.65%** of the handset market (figure from your notes; the 2024–25 figure is garbled).
- **Causes of decline:**
  - disruption by **Apple** (iPhone 2007, a closed but powerful ecosystem) and **Android**;
  - failure to read market trends;
  - slow commercialisation of its own innovations;
  - weak leadership, accountability and collaboration.
- **2011 "burning platform" memo** by CEO **Stephen Elop**: there were multiple fires, rivals moved faster than expected, Android had just taken Nokia's smartphone leadership, and Nokia had brilliant innovation inside but was not bringing it to market fast enough.

### 3.3 Why change is hard `[PN][SD]`
**A. Organisational inertia: five sources** `[PN]`:
| Source | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Organisational routines | Established ways of working |
| Social and political structures | Existing power relationships resist change |
| Conformity | Pressure to imitate established practices |
| Limited search | Failure to explore beyond familiar solutions |
| Complementarities and fit | Interdependent activities make isolated changes hard |

**B. Technological change** `[PN][SD]`:
| Type | Meaning | Authors |
|---|---|---|
| Competence-enhancing | Builds on existing competencies | Tushman & Anderson (1986) |
| Competence-destroying | Makes existing competencies obsolete | Tushman & Anderson (1986) |
| Component innovation | Changes individual components | Henderson & Clark (1990) |
| Architectural innovation | Changes how components are linked | Henderson & Clark (1990) |
| Disruptive technologies | Start inferior on mainstream metrics, then overturn established leaders | Christensen |

Nokia faced **architectural and competence-destroying** change: the smartphone as a software and ecosystem platform rather than hardware.

### 3.4 Five steps of turnaround `[PN][SD]`
1. **Change the leadership:** replace entrenched management.
2. **Redefine the strategic focus:** re-evaluate and rebuild the strategy.
3. **Asset sales and closures:** divest unwanted assets to free resources.
4. **Improve profitability:** cut costs, tighten financial and performance controls.
5. **Make acquisitions:** buy skills and competencies that strengthen the core.

### 3.5 Three types of turnaround / restructuring `[PN][SD]`
**A. Portfolio restructuring:** changing the configuration of business lines through acquisitions and divestitures.
| Downsizing | Downscoping |
|---|---|
| Fewer employees or units | Eliminate non-core businesses |
| Focus on cost reduction | Focus on the core business |
| Risk: losing human capital | Benefit: strategic focus |
| Example: Philips selling its TV business `[PN]` | Example: General Motors `[PN]` |

> Downsizing is **intentional and proactive**; decline is **involuntary**.

**B. Financial restructuring: the LBO** `[PN][SD]`:
- common in **mature industries** where R&D is not central;
- heavy debt commits cash flows to repayment, which **disciplines** management;
- concentrated ownership and active-investor boards give more oversight;
- a stronger focus on shareholder value;
- **higher financial risk**.

**MBO:** the existing managers buy the business.

**C. Organisational restructuring:**
- **restructuring managerial incentives:** align managers with the firm's goals;
- **strategic change:** redefine strategy and explore new opportunities.

Risk: disruption that destroys existing competencies.

### 3.6 Restructuring outcomes `[PN]` (Hitt et al.)
| Strategy | Short-term outcome | Long-term outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Downsizing | Lower labour costs | Loss of human capital → lower performance |
| Downscoping | Lower debt costs, stronger strategic controls | Higher performance |
| LBO | Stronger strategic controls, high debt costs | Higher performance, but higher risk |

These are *potential* outcomes, not guarantees.

### 3.7 Reading D: shrink, expand, neither, both? `[REC]` ⚠
Braun & Latham ask whether turnarounds should **retrench** (cut costs and assets), **expand** (invest, innovate, grow revenue) or combine the two. The usual lesson in turnaround research is that retrenchment stabilises the firm but rarely restores growth on its own. Successful comebacks often **combine** efficiency with a "recovery" strategy that reinvests in the core.

Use this as a discussion angle; verify it against the reading.

### 3.8 BlackBerry (optional case VI) `[SD]` + `[REC]`
- BlackBerry dominated enterprise smartphones but lost to touchscreen ecosystems.
- Its turnaround **downscoped** out of devices and refocused on its real capability: **secure enterprise software and network infrastructure** (cybersecurity, later IoT and automotive software).
- Managers quoted in the 2024–25 notes describe the devices as a "distraction" and the enterprise server as the real DNA.
- **Link to S5:** capabilities outlive products.

## 5. Comparisons
| A | B |
|---|---|
| Decline: involuntary | Downsizing: intentional |
| Downsizing: fewer people (cost) | Downscoping: fewer businesses (focus) |
| LBO: debt-financed, any buyer | MBO: bought by the managers |
| Competence-enhancing | Competence-destroying |
| Component innovation | Architectural innovation |
| Retrenchment (shrink) | Recovery / expansion (grow) |

## 6. Exam traps
- **Downsizing is not decline.** One is a deliberate strategy, the other an involuntary process.
- In the long run, downsizing tends to **hurt** performance (human capital); downscoping tends to **help**.
- An LBO's high debt is both the *source of discipline* and the *source of risk*.
- Nokia didn't lack innovation; it lacked **speed to market** and was hit by **architectural/ecosystem** change.
- The turnaround starts with **leadership change**.

## 8. Memorise this
- **Decline = involuntary erosion of performance and resources.**
- **Causes:** management · overexpansion · weak controls · high costs · new competition · demand shifts · inertia.
- **Inertia:** routines · socio-political structures · conformity · limited search · complementarities.
- **Technological change:** enhancing vs. destroying (Tushman & Anderson) · component vs. architectural (Henderson & Clark) · disruptive (Christensen).
- **Turnaround:** leadership → strategic focus → asset sales → profitability → acquisitions.
- **Restructuring:** portfolio (downsizing vs. downscoping) · financial (LBO/MBO) · organisational (incentives, strategic change).
- **Nokia = burning platform (Elop, 2011).**
