# S17 — Social and Environmental (Sustainable) Entrepreneurship

## 1. Learning objectives
- Define sustainable entrepreneurship and hybridity, and explain the forces behind it.
- Explain the theory of social entrepreneurship (Yunus; Santos 2012; market and government failure).
- Distinguish **integrated** from **differentiated** social enterprise models and the four types (BoP, MFI, FTPSE, WISE).
- Explain ecopreneurship and the extra blocks of a sustainable Business Model Canvas.
- Analyse the challenges: hybrid organising, mission drift, balancing mechanisms, scaling.
- Analyse the Allbirds case.

## 2. Core definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Sustainable entrepreneurship** | Entrepreneurship created to address an environmental or social problem; profit becomes a **means**, not the end, while staying financially sustainable. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Hybrid organisation** | An organisation that creates social and environmental value through **market mechanisms** (Doherty et al. 2014), combining divergent missions, goals, means and processes (Battilana & Lee 2014). `[PN][SD]` |
| **Social business** (Yunus) | A business that addresses a social problem while remaining financially sustainable. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Integrated model** | **Client = beneficiary:** the same activity generates revenue and impact. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Differentiated model** | **Client ≠ beneficiary:** revenue-generating and impact-generating activities are distinct. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Ecopreneurship** | Ecology + entrepreneurship: ventures that address ecological problems through entrepreneurial activity. `[PN][SD]` |
| **Mission drift** | The risk of pursuing one mission while neglecting the other. `[PN][SD]` |

## 3. Concepts explained

### 3.1 Why hybridity? `[PN][SD]`
**Drivers:**
- environmental disasters and climate change;
- growing social needs;
- the **interdependence of business and society**.

**The spectrum:**
- traditional charities on one end, traditional for-profit firms on the other;
- **social enterprises and B Corps** sit in the middle, combining social and financial returns.

**The trade-off** `[SD]`:
- combining environmental, social and economic missions is hard;
- not all customers will pay more for impact they don't directly perceive (fair trade);
- **environmental** benefits reach everyone, while **social** benefits target specific groups.

Sustainable enterprises are typically **created from scratch**; existing firms may *transform* through B Corp certification or Società Benefit status (see S16). `[SD]`

### 3.2 Social entrepreneurship theory `[PN][SD]`
**Why social enterprises exist** `[SD]`:
- **Market failure:** private firms can't profitably serve a need (private hospitals serve the wealthy).
- **Government failure:** public provision is inefficient (slow public hospitals).

Social enterprises fill the gap. Example: **Centro Medico Sant'Agostino**, low-price medical services with a lean, platform-based model.

**Santos (2012), four principles** `[PN]`:
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Value creation over value capture** | Prioritise benefits for users rather than profit maximisation |
| **Neglected problems** | Address problems caused by market and government failure |
| **Sustainable solutions** | Lasting solutions with positive externalities |
| **Empowerment over control** | Make beneficiaries independent rather than dependent |

### 3.3 Models of social enterprise `[PN][SD]`
| Model | Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BoP: Base of the Pyramid** | Integrated | Essential products and services (health, water, energy, finance, transport) at affordable prices for low-income customers | **M-Pesa** (mobile payments); **Totohealth** (maternal and child health, Kenya) |
| **MFI: Microfinance institution** | Integrated | Loans, savings and insurance for low-income people, often without collateral; "one-to-many" contracts with community guarantees | **Grameen Bank**; community-approval ("warden") micro-insurance `[SD]` |
| **FTPSE: Fair Trade Producer SE** | Differentiated | Generates income for marginalised producers by charging conscientious consumers a premium | **Nucafé** (Ugandan coffee farmers) |
| **WISE: Work Integration SE** | Differentiated | Employs vulnerable people at risk of exclusion from the labour market | **Wawoto Kacel** (marginalised women, Uganda); **PizzAut** (employees with autism); InGalera (prison restaurant) |

**Other cases** `[PN]`:
- **Zipline** (drone delivery of medical supplies);
- **Tugende** (asset finance for entrepreneurs);
- **Progetto Quid** (fashion made from surplus luxury fabrics, plus social inclusion);
- **Corec** (recycled construction materials plus inclusion);
- **Makers Unite** (products made from discarded life jackets);
- **Ocean Sole** (sculptures from discarded flip-flops);
- **Too Good To Go** (food-waste platform).

### 3.4 Ecopreneurship `[PN][SD]`
Environmental ventures that may also create social impact. Examples: **Allbirds**, Too Good To Go, Ocean Sole, Corec.

**Sustainable BMC: two extra blocks** `[SD]`:
- **Eco-social costs:**
  - What ecological or social costs does our model cause?
  - Which key resources are non-renewable?
  - Which activities consume the most resources?
- **Eco-social benefits:**
  - What benefits do we generate?
  - Who are the beneficiaries, and are they customers? Organic food: customer = beneficiary. Fair trade: the producers are the beneficiaries.
  - Can the benefits become part of a value proposition, and for whom?

### 3.5 Challenges `[PN][SD]`
**Hybrid organising** means managing social and commercial goals across five dimensions:
- inter-organisational relationships;
- organisational culture;
- organisational design;
- workforce composition;
- organisational activities.

**Mission drift** `[PN]` ⚠ (verify the alignment):
| | Financial mission drift | Social mission drift |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | Financial goals prioritised, social impact neglected | Social goals prioritised, financial sustainability neglected |
| Main risk | Loss of stakeholder **legitimacy** | **Bankruptcy** |
| More likely in | **Differentiated** SEs | **Integrated** SEs |

The 2024–25 notes stress the typical case: drifting toward profit at the expense of impact. `[SD]`

**Balancing mechanisms:**
- resource acquisition and allocation;
- team building;
- stakeholder engagement;
- growth strategies;
- performance measurement.

**Scaling** `[SD]`:
- **What it means:** growing customers or members and expanding the offer, potentially to millions of people.
- **Why scale:** a moral imperative, demand-side pressure, organisational needs, funder expectations, personal ambition.
- **Types:**
  - "scale deep": a new product to the same segment, or the same product to new customers;
  - "scale up": replication in other places, diversification.
- ⚠ These labels differ from the academic literature, where scaling out = replication, scaling up = institutional or policy change, and scaling deep = cultural change. Use the course labels, but be aware of the difference.

## 4. Case 8 — Allbirds (required) `[PN][SD]` + `[REC]`
**Context** `[REC]`:
- Founded in **2016** in San Francisco (Tim Brown and Joey Zwillinger).
- Sneakers made from **merino wool**, eucalyptus fibre and sugarcane-based foam.
- It published **carbon-footprint labels** on its products.
- It became a certified B Corp and a public benefit corporation, and went public in 2021.
- It then struggled: slowing sales, losses, a narrow product range and competition from mainstream brands. Verify the latest figures.

**Course reading:**
- **Ecopreneurship:** an environmental mission built into the core product. `[PN][SD]`
- **Type of innovation:** **incremental** rather than radical, applying wool to sneakers. `[SD]`
- **Sustainability strategy:** sustainable materials and carbon-footprint transparency, which is **sustainable branding** (S16) based on product-level differentiation. `[PN]`
- **Generic strategy:** differentiation (S7). Comfort and sustainability are intangible plus tangible differentiation.
- **Risks** `[REC]`:
  - sustainability claims are easy for big brands to copy (VRIO: imitable);
  - premium prices narrow demand;
  - the need to balance mission and growth (mission drift).

**Lesson:** a sustainability-based value proposition needs product excellence and hard-to-imitate resources to last.

## 6. Comparisons
| A | B |
|---|---|
| Integrated: client = beneficiary (BoP, MFI) | Differentiated: client ≠ beneficiary (FTPSE, WISE) |
| Financial mission drift → loss of legitimacy | Social mission drift → bankruptcy |
| Market failure | Government failure |
| Value creation | Value capture |
| Social enterprise (social mission) | Ecopreneurship (environmental mission) |

## 7. Exam traps
- **M-Pesa and Grameen = integrated**; **Nucafé and Wawoto Kacel = differentiated.**
- The **WISE** beneficiaries are the *employees*, not the customers.
- Mission drift can go **both** ways.
- **B Corp = certification**, not legal status (repeated from S16).
- Allbirds' innovation is **incremental**, not radical.
